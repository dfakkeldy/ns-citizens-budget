# Planning for AI

**A companion to my Budget 2027-28 submission to Building Our Budget Together**, Department of Finance and Treasury Board\
**From:** Dan Fakkeldy, Judique, Inverness County. **Date:** October 2026\
All figures are in Canadian dollars. Sources are numbered in brackets and listed at the end. A detailed assessment with calculations sits behind each ask; I am happy to send any of them.

---

## In one minute

- **Measured AI capability is rising fast, and official warnings have sharpened.** On METR's tests, mostly software, machine learning and cyber security tasks, the task length (in human-expert time) that frontier AI agents complete with 50% success doubled about every seven months from 2019 to 2025, and every four months since 2023 (January 2026 update) [1]. That does not show government productivity or staffing savings. In January 2024 the UK's cyber agency judged advanced AI attacks "unlikely" before 2025. In June 2026 the Five Eyes cyber agencies warned: "The timeline is not years, it is months" [2][3].
- **I am not asking the province to bet on AI.** I am asking it to avoid long lock-ins now and measure before scaling. Budget 2027-28 sets plans and baselines. The September 2027 forecast update checks baselines, notice and funding. Budget 2028-29 gets interim results. Pilots grow or stop later, on full results and provisional tests set in advance.
- **Eight asks, in priority order, cost about $3.1-7.7 million in 2027-28 (my estimates).** If a renewed AI line covers $1.3 million of it, about $1.8-6.4 million is new spending. No saving in my main submission pays for it, and I count no AI savings. Beyond cost recovery, the one new charge in my two papers (ask 2) applies only to qualifying new large loads of 25 MW or more; it is not levied on existing loads or ordinary household connections.

## The eight asks

### 1. Turn "workforce cuts, in part through AI" into an actual plan

- **What $95 million means.** The Budget Address promised civil-service cuts of "five per cent every year", saving "$95 million by year four" [4]. If that is the yearly saving in year four, at about $112,100 a funded position (my derivation [5]) it is about 850 positions, or about 2% a year.
- **Year one depends on the baseline.** The 2026-27 Estimates fund 605.6 fewer positions than the 2025-26 Estimates but only 117 fewer than the 2025-26 forecast, so most were probably vacant (my inference). Between the two Estimates, salary and benefits rose $90.6 million [5].
- **The released AI overview identifies no evaluation.** The 2026-27 AI line of $4.4 million is $0.75 million for salaries, $1.275 million for program development and $2.375 million for "Hardware, Software, and Licensing Fees" [6]. In April 2026 the deputy minister whose department runs it could not yet say which positions her department would cut, or give its attrition rate [7].
- **Ask:**
  - Publish the plan: job families in scope, attrition by department and how long tasks take.
  - Run three staggered pilots with comparison groups in the contact centre, FOIPOP processing and income-assistance casework, each assisting staff and never deciding. FOIPOP starts on closed files, with staff approving every redaction.
  - Check baselines, notice and funding at the September 2027 forecast update. Start pilots from October 1, 2027, and report interim results in Budget 2028-29. After two full quarters, extend a tool if staff time per task, from system logs, falls 15% or more against its comparison group with no rise in errors, complaints or appeals; stop it if those rise (my provisional test). AI benchmarks [1] do not show staffing savings.
  - Review vacancies in highly exposed job families for redesign before refilling them, except front-line, rural, inspection and enforcement roles, as in my main submission.
  - Report funded and filled positions quarterly by department and job classification.
  - Fund an independent evaluation, and retrain and redeploy staff under the existing Memorandum #2 [8].
  - Seek a letter now with the NSGEU on when AI counts as technological change, as British Columbia did in 2025 [9]. If an October 1, 2027 pilot is technological change (clause 37.02), it needs six months' notice, by April 1, 2027 (clause 37.04) [8].
- **Cost:** $1.2-3.2 million (my estimate). It tests whether my submission's attrition rows ($24 million and $6 million in 2027-28) can be delivered without layoffs. That needs enough suitable vacancies and no loss of service.

### 2. Set the price of data-centre power before anyone signs

- **The requests are large, and the grid plan leaves them out.** IESO Nova Scotia is studying 650 MW of large-load requests in Richmond County, about 26% of the 2026 forecast peak. Neither the customers nor their uses are named [10]. The grid plan's draft high scenario models only 210 MW of new large loads, as "illustrative proxies" [11].
- **Halifax's benchmark power price is comparatively high.** For large-power customers (5,000 kW, 3,060,000 kWh a month), Halifax had the highest average price before taxes of 12 Canadian cities in Hydro-Québec's April 2025 comparison: 13.43 cents a kWh, against 12.80 in Toronto and 5.83 in Montréal [44]. That may prompt requests for subsidies, a special rate or a direct wind supply (my inference), which is why the terms come first.
- **A local precedent fell short.** An April 2025 Energy Board decision records consultant Bates White's view that benefits to other customers from Port Hawkesbury Paper's special tariff were about 56% lower than expected in 2020-2023 [12]. Its proposed replacement (matter M12661) was still undecided on October 3, 2026 [13].
- **Ask:**
  - No provincial grants, equity, guarantees or discounted power for data centres.
  - A rate class for qualifying new large loads (25 MW or more, including expansions of that size at an existing site), with minimum bills, collateral and exit fees, so they pay the costs they create. Ohio's data-centre tariff commits customers for up to 12 years at up to 85% minimum demand, with a 36-month exit fee [14].
  - A price condition for the same qualifying loads: a per-MWh charge that cannot be offset against corporate tax, as the direct return above normal taxes the Premier sought in September 2026 [15]. Beyond cost recovery, it is the one new charge in my two papers. It applies only to qualifying new large loads; it is not levied on existing loads or ordinary household connections. At an illustrative $4-10 per MWh, a 325 MW load would pay about $10-24 million a year (my arithmetic, at 85% average use).
  - A municipal planning framework.
  - Book $0 of data-centre revenue until a load is connected.
- **Cost:** $0.5-0.9 million. If capacity built for a 325 MW customer sat idle, other customers could pay about $93-135 per account a year (my estimate).

### 3. Cyber security: pay by results

- **The patch standard released under FOIPOP in February 2025 dates from 2013.** It allows two weeks for Windows servers and quarterly patching for some systems. Legacy systems "are not required to comply immediately" [16]. A newer one may exist. A June 2026 US federal directive sets three days for the riskiest known-exploited flaws [17].
- **Hiring may limit results more than money.** The cyber unit gets $17.4 million in 2026-27. Its 2025-26 spending was forecast at 14% under budget. In 2024-25 its department was 11% under funded staffing, "due to delays in filling vacancies" [5][18].
- **Ask:**
  - Keep my main submission's protection of the unit, but publish its results.
  - Publish the current patch standard and set three days for exposed, known-exploited flaws.
  - Point $5-10 million of existing capital at internet-facing legacy systems first.
  - An incident-response retainer open to health, schools and municipalities.
  - Minimum security clauses in every IT and ed-tech contract.
  - A small, two-year AI-defence pilot against existing tools, with a decision at month 12: stop if it finds no more confirmed flaws than current tools, counting false positives and staff time.
- **Cost:** $0.1-0.5 million of new money.

### 4. Put an AI scenario in the fiscal plan

- **Most revenue depends on wages and spending.** Personal income tax, HST, corporate income tax and Equalization are 69.8% of 2026-27 revenue [19].
- **A stress test, not a forecast (my model).** If the wage share of the economy fell 0.5 points a year from 2027-28, about the pace of the UK fiscal watchdog's illustrative scenario [20], revenue would be about $190 million a year lower by 2031-32 after a partial Equalization offset. At 1 point a year, about $380 million. The model holds GDP fixed, so it leaves out any AI productivity gain, and uses five assumed parameters. Equalization responds only to Nova Scotia's loss relative to other provinces, two to four years later.
- **Others already do this.** The UK's fiscal watchdog, New York City's Comptroller, Australia's Treasury and the IMF published AI scenarios in 2026 [20]-[23]. The published Budget 2026-27 has no AI downside scenario [19].
- **Ask:** an AI paragraph and a labour-share stress test in the risk section; the labour share of GDP added to the key revenue variables; triggers set now.
- **Cost:** about $0.

### 5. FOIPOP: ready for April 1, 2027

- **The review office spends more than its budget.** It spent $1.70 million in 2025-26 against $1.33 million budgeted. Its 2026-27 budget of $1.45 million is about 14% below forecast spending of $1.68 million [24]. Estimates forecasts show $1.27 million of its costs charged to other departments from 2021-22 to 2025-26 [5]. The office's own actual figures differ [24].
- **The new Act starts with a backlog under the old one.** About 540 review files will be open on April 1, 2027 (my estimate). The transition rules keep them under the old Act [25].
- **A registry pre-search could ease part of the Environment load.** In 2025-26, Environment got 48% of all requests [26]. Its guidance sends contamination searches to FOIPOP ($5) rather than its own registry ($26.50) [27]. Ontario's $65 pre-search, running since 2022, says whether records exist in three days on average. Copies still need an access request [28].
- **Ask:**
  - As in my main submission: about $1.7 million for the review office, subject to workload costing, and neutral only if Finance transfers equal funding now budgeted elsewhere.
  - A three-year team for the old-Act files (about $0.32 million a year).
  - Publish repeatedly requested Environment lists, and add a registry pre-search at no more than today's $26.50 fee.
  - Report suspected AI-generated requests and appeals quarterly, and how they were flagged. AI use alone should not justify refusing a request. In Scotland, 34% of 352 appeals received between August and October 2025 were found AI-generated [29].
- **Cost:** $0.4-0.6 million (my estimate). The review office budget is in my main submission. The AI processing pilot is counted in ask 1.

### 6. Short, exit-ready AI contracts and office leases

- **New long leases while cuts are planned.** In calendar 2026, Public Works awarded four office leases worth $57.1 million ($12.1 million in 2025-26, $45.0 million in 2026-27), two stated as 10-year terms [30]. They may replace space the province already rents. Core government's commitments under all its operating leases, not only offices, were $372.7 million at March 31, 2026 (before the 2026-27 awards), up 50% in two years [31].
- **AI contracts are already being signed.** I found six AI-related awards since October 2025, worth $8.0 million, five without competition [32]. The procurement policy (November 2024 version) refers to the privacy laws but says nothing about AI, training use of data, portability or exit [40].
- **There is a deadline.** Contracts handling personal information and committed by April 30, 2027 keep the old in-Canada rules for their whole term [25].
- **Ask:**
  - A standard AI contract schedule in force before May 1, 2027: no training on provincial data without consent, stated storage location, audit rights, notice of model changes, usable data hand-over, termination for convenience, no minimum spend and a year-two renewal decision.
  - Put it in new solicitations before bids close, in non-competitive awards, and in existing contracts at renewal or by agreement. The closed Microsoft licensing-partner tender must follow its published terms (rule 9 of my submission).
  - No office lease over five years without a year-five break until a space plan is published, and publish the lease inventory.
  - Review points before the business registry and PowerSchool renewals.
- **Cost:** $0-0.3 million.

### 7. Schools: use the bargaining round, and fix classroom assessment

- **A new teachers' agreement is due.** The current one ran to August 31, 2026 and stays in force until replaced [33]. At its 2026 Annual Council the NSTU adopted a policy that AI "must not replace any Member", that teachers may opt in or not, and that they keep "the final authority on evaluating student progress". It also resolved to seek an AI policy for schools written jointly with the department [34]. The asks below are my proposals.
- **The risk is unsupervised classroom work.** Provincial tests are already supervised [35]. In a 2024 study at one UK university, 94% of AI-written answers to at-home exams in five undergraduate psychology modules went undetected [36]. It is not a detection rate for Nova Scotia or today's AI.
- **The assessment cut is unexplained.** The 2026-27 Assessment line is 31% below the 2025-26 estimate (26% below the forecast), and the Estimates give no reason [5]. British Columbia and Québec publish provincial AI guidance for schools [41]; I found none for Nova Scotia (October 3, 2026).
- **Ask:**
  - A joint AI, workload and assessment committee, set up by a letter of understanding.
  - Provincial guidance written with the NSTU: teachers opt in, and each AI tool gets a privacy review first.
  - Explain the cut, publish the missing Grade 10 results [35], and fund supervised classroom tasks.
  - A randomized Grade 8 math pilot of a hint-based tutor (it gives hints, not answers) in 2027-28, in class with the teacher present, after a privacy review, with a pre-registered evaluation sized to detect about 0.12 standard deviations. Report usage and safety before Budget 2028-29. Judge it on the May 2028 provincial test, written without AI, and decide in Budget 2029-30. Provisional rule: expand only if that evaluation credibly shows a gain of 0.12 standard deviations or more with no harm; pull back on harm or a privacy incident.
- **Cost:** $0.8-1.45 million. No teaching position is added or cut.

### 8. Digital-first, with a rural floor

- **Front-line staff are being cut, with no yardstick.** Citizen Services, which runs the counters and phones, loses 38.1 funded positions in 2026-27 (−9.6% from its 2025-26 forecast), after a $3.3 million overrun in 2025-26 [5][42]. Service NS's 2024-25 accountability report gives no wait times [37], and I found no published phone or counter standard (October 3, 2026).
- **Older people use online services much less.** In 2022, 55.4% of Canadians aged 65 and older used government services online, against 90.1% of those aged 25-44 [38]. On the province's Access Nova Scotia and motor-vehicle office lists (October 3, 2026), Port Hawkesbury is the only open office in Inverness County [43].
- **Ask:**
  - Publish service standards (calls, in-person waits, distance), as Service Canada already does. It met its 50 km standard 96.6% of the time in 2024-25 [39].
  - Assisted digital service, with scheduled outreach days. Three Inverness County sites twice a month: about $77,000 a year (my estimate).
  - No closure justified by a digital channel until completion rates for older and rural users are measured.
- **Cost:** $0.1-0.7 million.

## Questions I would like Budget 2027-28 to answer

1. Does "five per cent every year" compound? How is "$95 million by year four" calculated, and how many filled positions were removed in 2026-27?
2. What will the renewed AI budget measure, and which jobs and tasks is it meant to change?
3. What price, contract length and exit terms will apply to very large new power customers, and will the province offer any financial support?
4. Has the 2013 patch standard been replaced, will current patch deadlines be published, and what results will the $17.4 million cyber unit publish?
5. Has Finance run any AI or automation scenario on revenue, and will it publish it?
6. Will the review office's 2027-28 budget include funding now charged to other departments, and how will the old-Act backlog be cleared?
7. What are the terms and break rights of the 2026 office leases, including the 71,992-square-foot lease, and what space do they replace?
8. Why was the Assessment line cut 31% from its 2025-26 estimate, and when will 2024-25 Grade 10 results be published?

Thank you for reading.
