# Homes and growth

## Housing

Housing starts reached a record in 2025 but fell in the first eight months of 2026, and rent-supplement support has expanded [12][48]. But average two-bedroom rents in private apartments rose about 37% in both the Halifax and Cape Breton census areas from October 2021 to October 2025 [23], the public housing waitlist was 8,043 in June 2026 [52], and the public housing agency's spring 2026 business plan reported 51 of 515 new units complete; the fall update reports 307 complete or underway at June 30 without separating the two [11][12]. Two deadlines fall inside 2027-28: the rent cap ends on 31 December 2027 [56], and the Canada-Nova Scotia housing agreement ends on 31 March 2028 [54].

::: {.rail label="Public land"}
**Ready-to-Build Public Land.** Land for Housing has 14 projects conditionally approved that could create 1,202 units; the two completed so far have delivered 35 [12][24]. List every provincial parcel inside existing water and sewer boundaries, excluding licensed, protected, coastal-recreation and Mi'kmaq negotiation lands. Do the wetland, servicing and zoning work first. Consult the Mi'kmaq of Nova Scotia on the whole list through a process agreed with them, with site-specific consultation wherever rights may be affected, and offer Mi'kmaw communities first choice of parcels near them. Release parcels for $1 or on a lease that converts to ownership only once homes are built and lived in, with buy-back if not. Open it to small local builders, co-ops and non-profits, plus a pilot of 10 to 20 household lots a year in serviced villages like mine, by lottery among anyone who will build and live there. Existing land-disposal authority can be used, with the required Cabinet approvals, a published program policy and site-specific legal and consultation checks [25]. Publish a quarterly scorecard.
:::

::: {.rail label="Rent"}
**Make rent protection permanent before the cap ends.** No current tenant loses protection. Use an annual guideline tied to inflation, with a ceiling no higher than today's 5%, and limit using the end of a fixed-term lease to remove a sitting tenant. To keep new rentals coming, units first occupied after a set date would be exempt; I state that openly because it narrows today's rule.
:::

::: {.rail label="Federal money"}
**Lock in federal housing money** before the agreement ends, including a named rural share of Build Canada Homes funding.
:::

::: {.rail label="Public housing"}
**Use the public housing we already own.** Finish the Auditor General's 2022 recommendations, about half done at March 2026, including lease-renewal eligibility checks [55]. The agency head told CBC in July 2026 that right-sizing was 14% complete, with more than 1,000 households still over-housed [52]. I propose a voluntary target of at least half, with same-community options and published progress. Where existing rentals are cheap, as in much of Cape Breton, consider buying and preserving them before building new, within existing capital room.
:::

::: {.rail label="Planning"}
**Housing-first planning rules for every serviced town**, extending HRM's 2024 rules: four units as of right on serviced lots, no more than one parking space per unit for small buildings, pre-approved federal catalogue designs, and a decision deadline for complete permits. Inverness County allows up to four units per lot in its residential centre and hamlet zones but requires 1.5 parking spaces per unit [26].
:::

::: {.rail label="Suites"}
**Lend, don't give.** Reopen the secondary and backyard suite program, which closed in March 2026 after beating its target [57], as repayable loans at the Province's borrowing cost, with a stream for small rentals outside HRM. Start with a pilot once Finance has costed its cap, administration and loan losses.
:::

::: {.rail label="Supportive housing"}
**Supportive housing for rural Cape Breton.** Extend the September 2026 call for community-led supportive housing to Inverness, Richmond and Victoria counties and CBRM, using new or unallocated room in the existing $165.2 million budget. The June 2024 service-based count found 87 people aged 16 and over experiencing or at risk of homelessness in those three counties, including 10 unsheltered and 5 in emergency shelter [50].
:::

::: {.rail label="Finished homes"}
**Count homes finished, by region.** The 2023 needs assessment estimated about 590 homes a year were needed in the Cape Breton region from 2023 to 2027, including catching up on the existing shortfall, against a status-quo pace of about 150 a year [27]. Protect the trades that finish them: Plan B makes no further cut to apprenticeship or skills training.
:::

::: {.rail label="Rental rebate"}
**Keep the rental HST rebate; reconcile and evaluate it** before any change. It is estimated at $95.1 million in 2026-27 [3].
:::

## Growing the economy

::: {.rail label="Newcomers"}
**Keep the people we recruit.** For immigrants admitted in 2017, five-year retention was 62.1% in Nova Scotia and 93.5% in Ontario [28]. Within federal allocations, weight nominations by measured retention by stream and region, require settlement support from endorsed employers, and publish retention every year.
:::

::: {.rail label="Technology"}
**Technology adoption through services, not a tax break.** Nova Scotia small businesses already pay a 1.5% rate on their first $700,000 of income [3], so a non-refundable credit is worth little to them. Instead:

- refocus Invest NS's Productivity and Innovation Voucher Program as a $15,000 to $25,000 technology-adoption voucher for firms under 100 staff, with a rural and Cape Breton share (in spring 2026, 9.9% of rural and 21.0% of urban businesses in Canada had used AI in the past year [29]);
- add a small-project stream inside the existing $17.0 million Innovation Rebate Program for fish plants, mills and manufacturers;
- place advisers at NSCC and Cape Breton University outside Halifax, with federal co-funding sought;
- require three years of Nova Scotia payroll maintenance, with a clawback, and a worker training plan, and end the program on 31 March 2030 after an evaluation.

The UK's Made Smarter evaluation did not detect a statistically significant employment effect and found only delayed, uncertain modelled productivity gains [61]. So productivity is the target, with no revenue booked.
:::

::: {.rail label="Incentives"}
**Evaluate every business incentive** independently every three to five years. Programs expire unless renewed, tax credits get a review date, and per-deal results are published.
:::

::: {.rail label="Permits"}
**Fewer Permits, Faster Projects, with legal clocks.** If a minister misses a statutory deadline, the expert assessor's recommendation becomes the decision. No approval without a completed assessment, and the clock stops during Crown consultation with the Mi'kmaq.
:::

::: {.rail label="Windfalls"}
**Windfalls to debt.** Resource revenue above trend, such as offshore wind leases and royalties, goes first to debt while the Province runs deficits. In 2005, Nova Scotia used its $830 million offshore payment to retire debt [30]. Any provincial guarantee or equity stake in Wind West gets independent review, and any commitment on ratepayers goes to the Nova Scotia Energy Board [11].
:::

::: {.rail label="Seafood"}
**Seafood before the tariff cliff.** China's tariff suspension ends on 31 December 2026 [1]. Use existing market-diversification money with the federal Trade Commissioner Service and ACOA now, and federal money before provincial for dual-use projects such as the Halifax and Sydney ports.
:::
